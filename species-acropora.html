Acropora

Acropora is one of the most diverse and widespread coral genera, with over 150 species.

Morphology

Colonies form intricate branching, table, or corymbose shapes with a prominent axial corallite and tightly packed radial corallites that create sharp tips. Thin coenosteum walls and pastel growth bands reveal seasonal calcification pulses.

Distribution

Acropora dominates well-lit reef flats and upper slopes throughout the Indo-Pacific, with a handful of Caribbean endemics. Most species occupy water shallower than 15 m where wave energy and currents deliver oxygenated water.

Ecological Role

Fast skeletal accretion makes Acropora the primary framework builder on many reefs. Its thickets provide nurseries for juvenile fish, stabilize sediments, and baffle waves, boosting shoreline protection.

Conservation Status

Multiple species, including Acropora palmata and cervicornis, are listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Thermal stress, white-band disease, and crown-of-thorns outbreaks have removed >80% of some regional populations since the 1980s. Restoration relies on fragment nurseries, assisted gene flow, and strict marine protected areas to maintain genetic diversity.
