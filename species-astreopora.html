Astreopora

Astreopora is a genus of stony corals characterized by their encrusting to massive growth forms. These corals feature distinctive star-shaped corallites and are important reef builders in many tropical regions.

Morphology

Astreopora polyps form star-shaped calices only a few millimeters wide, linked by porous coenosteum that keeps colonies lightweight. Surfaces often show alternating ridges and shallow pits that trap fine sediments.

Distribution

This genus spans the entire Indo-Pacific from the Red Sea to French Polynesia, thriving between 3 and 25 meters on fore-reef slopes, turbid lagoons, and occasionally deeper mesophotic ledges.

Ecological Role

Massive colonies cement patch-reef frameworks and their porous skeletons create refuge for boring sponges and small motile invertebrates, enhancing microhabitat diversity on otherwise smooth reef faces.
