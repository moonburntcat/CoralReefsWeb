Sinularia

Sinularia is a genus of soft corals, also known as finger leather corals, characterized by their branching, finger-like projections. These flexible corals add structural diversity to reef ecosystems without contributing to the rigid reef framework.

Morphology

Sinularia colonies grow as thick, finger-like lobes supported by microscopic sclerites, with polyps emerging from the lobes’ surfaces to form a velvety texture under water flow.

Distribution

This soft-coral genus dominates shallow lagoonal habitats across the Indo-Pacific, especially on turbid reefs of the South China Sea, Coral Triangle, and northern Australia.

Ecological Role

By carpeting large areas, Sinularia stabilizes sediment, hosts nitrogen-fixing microbes, and releases allelopathic chemicals that influence the balance between soft and hard corals.

Conservation Status

Generally abundant, yet susceptible to collection for aquarium trade and bio-prospecting. Polluted runoff can clog polyps and reduce photosynthesis, leading to tissue necrosis. Turquoise soft-coral reserves in Indonesia now limit harvest and protect source populations.
