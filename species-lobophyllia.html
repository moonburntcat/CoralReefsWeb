Lobophyllia

Lobophyllia is a genus of large polyp stony corals known for their fleshy polyps and distinctive lobed appearance. These corals form substantial colonies with prominent, often colorful polyps that create a striking visual presence on the reef.

Morphology

Lobophyllia polyps grow into thick fleshy lobes separated by deep valleys, with septa lined by large teeth that give living tissue a sculpted appearance under low-angle light.

Distribution

Found from the Red Sea to the Western Pacific, this genus prefers protected reef slopes and lagoons between 3 and 40 meters, often anchoring on consolidated rubble or vertical faces.

Ecological Role

The broad lobes trap organic detritus and provide microhabitats for commensal shrimps and brittle stars, while their slow growth fills gaps between faster branching corals, boosting structural heterogeneity.
