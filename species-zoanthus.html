Zoanthus

Zoanthus is a genus of colonial anemones, often called zoanthids or button polyps, that form colorful mats on reef substrates. These organisms are closely related to corals and add vibrant colors to reef ecosystems.

Morphology

Zoanthus forms button-like polyps connected by a coenenchyme mat; each polyp displays concentric color rings and a fringe of tentacles surrounding the mouth.

Distribution

Colonies carpet shallow reef flats, mangrove roots, and lagoonal boulders across tropical Indo-Pacific and Atlantic regions, tolerating both high light and turbid settings.

Ecological Role

Dense mats stabilize rubble, recycle particulate organic matter through active feeding, and provide habitat for small crustaceans that graze among the polyps.
