Pocillopora

Pocillopora is a genus of branching stony corals known for their bushy, cauliflower-like appearance. These fast-growing corals are important early colonizers of reef areas and play a crucial role in reef development.

Morphology

Pocillopora forms cauliflower-like clumps with short branches covered in wart-like verrucae that house small polyps. Colonies range from spherical heads to flattened plates depending on flow.

Distribution

The genus spans tropical and subtropical Indo-Pacific reefs, thriving from shallow surge zones to mesophotic habitats up to 40 meters deep, with some species reaching the eastern Pacific.

Ecological Role

As a pioneer coral, Pocillopora quickly colonizes disturbed substrates, provides nursery shelter for damselfish and cardinalfish, and stabilizes rubble so slower-growing corals can settle.
