Xenia

Xenia soft corals are famous for their pulsing polyps that rhythmically open and close, driving gentle currents that keep reef flats oxygenated. Their feathery tentacles shimmer in daylight, forming waving meadows across shallow lagoons.

Morphology

Each stalk ends in eight feathery tentacles that pulse thanks to contractile tissue bands. Colonies spread through stolons that quickly encrust live rock, creating low, bushy mats.

Distribution

Xenia thrives on sunlit back-reef flats from the Red Sea to Micronesia, tolerating fluctuating salinity and temperatures between 23–30 °C.

Ecological Role

Pulsing polyps enhance water exchange, helping remove mucus and sediments from neighboring corals. Their rapid growth offers shelter for amphipods and juvenile shrimps.
