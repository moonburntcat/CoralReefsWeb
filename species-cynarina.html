Cynarina

Cynarina is a genus of large polyp stony corals known for their solitary or small colonial forms. These corals feature a single, large polyp that can expand dramatically, creating a striking appearance on the reef.

Morphology

Cynarina displays solitary, disk-shaped corallites with a fleshy mantle that inflates into translucent lobes. Radiating septa create grooves visible through the tissue, and the central mouth often shows contrasting color rings.

Distribution

The genus occurs from the Red Sea and East Africa to the Central Pacific, favoring sandy pockets on reef slopes, lagoon floors, and shaded crevices between 5 and 40 meters depth.

Ecological Role

As nocturnal suspension feeders, Cynarina polyps capture plankton that would otherwise escape reef frameworks, recycling organic matter and offering feeding grounds for nocturnal fishes and crustaceans.
