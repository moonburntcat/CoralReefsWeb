Lobophytum

Lobophytum is a genus of soft corals, also known as leather corals, characterized by their fleshy, lobed appearance and flexible structure. These corals lack a rigid skeleton and instead have a leathery texture.

Structure

Unlike stony corals, Lobophytum has a flexible, leathery structure without a rigid calcium carbonate skeleton, allowing for movement with water flow.

Growth Form

Colonies form lobed, finger-like projections that can cover large areas, creating distinctive textures on the reef substrate.

Chemical Defense

These soft corals produce chemical compounds that deter predators and can inhibit the growth of nearby stony corals.

Ecological Role

  • Adds structural diversity to reef ecosystems with flexible, three-dimensional forms.
  • Chemical compounds have potential pharmaceutical applications in medical research.
  • Competitive interactions with stony corals influence reef community composition.
