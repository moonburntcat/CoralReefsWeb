Sarcophyton

Sarcophyton is a genus of soft corals, commonly known as leather corals or toadstool corals, characterized by their fleshy, mushroom-like appearance. These corals lack a rigid skeleton and have a distinctive leathery texture.

Structure

The mushroom-like or toadstool shape, with a central stalk and expanded cap, is characteristic of Sarcophyton and gives it its common name.

Flexibility

Without a rigid skeleton, Sarcophyton colonies can move with water currents, reducing damage from strong wave action.

Chemical Compounds

These corals produce bioactive compounds that have potential applications in pharmaceutical research and can deter predators.

Ecological Role

  • Adds structural diversity to reef ecosystems with flexible, three-dimensional forms.
  • Chemical compounds are being studied for potential medical applications.
  • Popular in reef aquariums due to their distinctive appearance and relative hardiness.
