Sarcophyton

Sarcophyton is a genus of soft corals, commonly known as leather corals or toadstool corals, characterized by their fleshy, mushroom-like appearance. These corals lack a rigid skeleton and have a distinctive leathery texture.

Morphology

Sarcophyton forms a leathery stalk topped with a mushroom-shaped cap whose surface hosts retractable polyps. Internal sclerites reinforce the tissue while allowing the colony to flex.

Distribution

Common throughout the Indo-Pacific, toadstool leather corals dominate shallow reef flats and lagoonal bommies between 1 and 20 meters, often thriving in turbid water where hard corals struggle.

Ecological Role

The broad caps baffle currents, trap suspended particles, and offer perches for juvenile fishes. Allelopathic chemicals deter competitors and shape community patterns around soft-coral meadows.
