Porites

Porites is one of the most important reef-building coral genera, forming massive colonies that can live for centuries. These corals are known for their resilience and are among the most common corals in many reef systems.

Morphology

Porites exhibits small, closely spaced corallites embedded in a smooth coenosteum, forming massive boulders, finger-like branches, or thin crusts depending on species and flow regime.

Distribution

One of the most widespread genera, Porites dominates Indo-Pacific reefs and extends into some Atlantic locales, thriving from shallow flats to mesophotic depths beyond 60 meters.

Ecological Role

Long-lived colonies provide stable reef framework, record centuries of ocean chemistry, and host specialized symbionts such as Porites-associated crabs that defend against predators.
