Galaxea

Galaxea is a genus of stony corals known for their distinctive star-shaped corallites and aggressive growth. These corals form large, encrusting to massive colonies that can dominate reef areas.

Morphology

Galaxea colonies form low mounds covered in dense tubular corallites whose sharp septa create star-like oral discs. Nighttime extension of long sweeper tentacles gives colonies a fuzzy halo.

Distribution

The genus inhabits shallow reef flats and upper slopes across the Indo-Pacific, particularly in the Coral Triangle and Great Barrier Reef, usually between 2 and 20 meters depth.

Ecological Role

By rapidly colonizing open substrate, Galaxea stabilizes rubble beds after storms while its sweeper tentacles regulate spacing with neighboring corals, shaping local community mosaics.

Conservation Status

Most species remain of Least Concern but are sensitive to sedimentation and thermal stress. Coastal development that boosts nutrient loads can favor algae over Galaxea, reducing recruitment. Marine protected areas help maintain herbivore populations that keep settlement surfaces free of macroalgae.
