Acanthastrea

Acanthastrea is a genus of large polyp stony corals known for their vibrant colors and distinctive corallite structures. These corals form massive colonies with prominent, often colorful polyps.

Morphology

Large meandroid corallites share thin walls and heavy septa whose teeth create the scalloped ridges seen on living tissue. Polyps swell into fleshy lobes with fluorescent pigments that shift under different light spectra.

Distribution

Acanthastrea occupies sheltered reef slopes, back-reef lagoons, and turbid nearshore platforms from the Red Sea through the Central Pacific, typically between 3 and 30 meters depth.

Ecological Role

The thick tissue resists partial predation and shades underlying skeleton, allowing colonies to stabilize sediment pockets and provide crevice habitat for cryptic crustaceans and juvenile fishes.
